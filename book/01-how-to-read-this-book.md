# How to Read This Book

This book has one purpose: to help you get hired as a forward deployed AI engineer. It teaches every concept inside one client engagement, because the interview tests situations, not definitions.

```
Getting hired as a forward deployed AI engineer
├── What the interview tests:   situations, not definitions
├── One client:                 the Mehra & Cole engagement
├── How the book is organized:  Prologue, Case, three Parts, Epilogue
└── How to read it:             in order, deciding before you read on
```

Each section below fills in one branch.

## What the interview tests

Clients have messy, specific problems no product solves out of the box. The job is working out which ideas fit this client, this data and these people. So the interview rarely asks for a definition. It hands you a situation and watches what you do.

- **A vague brief:** automate a process nobody has fully described. Where do you start?
- **A failing system:** it worked in the demo but fails on the client's real documents. Why?
- **A skeptical expert:** how do you know the model is good enough to show them?
- **An awkward room:** a senior client just embarrassed by an AI tool, or an executive who wants a promise you cannot make.

Each tests whether you can recognize which concept applies, use it under real constraints, and explain it without jargon. A candidate who learned concepts in isolation answers with a lecture. One who learned them in context answers like someone who has been in the room.

## One client, start to finish

Every concept is taught inside one fictional engagement, drawn from how real ones run. It is the reason each chapter exists, not an illustration.

- **The client:** Mehra & Cole LLP, a mid-size law firm in Mumbai, Delhi, Bengaluru and New York. Its corporate practice is losing money and pitches on legal due diligence, the document review before every acquisition.
- **You:** the forward deployed engineer Inherent Labs sends after the managing partner hires it.
- **The setup:** a signed statement of work, a steering committee, twelve weeks, a security review, skeptical partners, overworked associates, disorganized data rooms and an agreed business goal.
- **How concepts arrive:** you learn how language models work because a chatbot invented case law for an associate. You learn retrieval because a key clause sits in an amendment letter in the wrong folder.

So in every main chapter, the situation comes first and the concept follows. That is the order concepts arrive on an engagement and in an interview, where nobody says which chapter a question belongs to. (The front matter, including this page, opens with its main point instead.)

**Why a law firm.** It is a hard place to deploy AI. The documents are long, inconsistent and multilingual. A missed clause is expensive. The users are experts who will not trust what they cannot check, and the data is confidential, subject to residency rules and ethical walls. The system must cut the hours a report takes, not just demo well. If you can reason through Mehra & Cole, you can reason through the hospital or bank in your interview.

## How the book is organized

Two short pieces set the scene. Then three Parts each answer one question every forward deployed engineer faces, in the order the engagement raises them.

- **Prologue:** what a forward deployed engineer is, and how the role differs from its neighbors.
- **The Case:** the engagement up to the morning it becomes yours, ending with a reference section of people, numbers, phases and meetings.
- **Part I, Build (Chapters 1–7):** can you make it work? It ends with a working prototype and an unmoved client.
- **Part II, Land (Chapters 8–13):** can you make them believe it? It ends with a client who trusts you, and so depends on you.
- **Part III, Lead (Chapters 14–18):** can it survive without you?
- **Epilogue:** the decision gate, and a map from each Part to the interview.

Each Part ends with the problem the next must solve. That chain is the argument of the book.

**Every main chapter has the same shape.** It opens where the last one left the engagement, introduces one central image, explains the idea from first principles, then returns to Mehra & Cole to show what you say or do. It closes with the problem the next chapter must solve. Technical chapters add Further reading. Each concept is explained once and then used by name, so the chapters depend on each other.

## How to read it

Read the way you would work the engagement.

- **Read in order:** a chapter that seems to start abruptly is picking up a thread.
- **Treat the Case as a briefing pack:** know the people, promises and open questions. Look things up in its reference section.
- **Decide before you read on:** when a scene sets up a situation, decide what you would do. The gap between your answer and the chapter's is what an interviewer looks for.
- **Read the papers:** interviewers often know the primary sources.
- **Do not skip Parts II and III:** many strong engineers fail there, because the interview weighs judgment as seriously as code.

By the end, a messy interview scenario should feel familiar, because you have already lived a version of it: twelve weeks at a law firm in Mumbai.
